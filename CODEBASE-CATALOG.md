> **For prospective buyers, product teams and founders.** This catalogue is an intake point for licensing, white-label work, source-code acquisition discussions and deployment/engineering services. ## How to work with a codebase

| Option | Best for | What gets agreed before payment |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **License or source access** | Reusing an existing foundation under permitted rights | Exact repo/commit, use rights, exclusions, third-party licenses, support terms |
| **White-label / customize** | Adapting product UX, brand, flows, APIs or business rules | Included changes, acceptance criteria, delivery milestone, environment and deployment assumptions |
| **Source acquisition / buyout discussion** | Exploring an ownership or transfer transaction | Ownership chain, existing licenses, third-party assets, transfer scope, exclusivity if legally available, signed agreement |
| **Deployment and handoff** | Getting an agreed build running in the buyer's accounts | Hosting, secrets, CI/CD, domain, store/cloud access, docs, handover session |
| **Technical due diligence** | Understanding a repo before investing further | Architecture review, setup/build check, dependencies, security/privacy surfaces, known gaps and estimate |
| **Custom implementation** | Extending a base or building a defined product slice | Milestones, budget, timeline, IP terms and ongoing support |

**Important licensing note:** code that is publicly visible on GitHub is not automatically available for commercial reuse or exclusive purchase. The existing LICENSE file (if any), open-source dependencies, fonts, artwork, model licenses and service terms govern their own parts. A previously granted open-source license cannot simply be withdrawn from existing recipients. I confirm what rights can actually be offered before quoting a transaction. This is a commercial workflow note, not legal advice.
